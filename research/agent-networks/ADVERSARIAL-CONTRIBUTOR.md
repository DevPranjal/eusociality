# Next development question: a contributing adversary

The completed-message comparison will not be treated as general compromise
resistance. Its adversary can send guidance but cannot author candidates. A
separately preregistered development study will address that missing intervention.

## Question and threat boundary

**When one registered contributor deliberately proposes a contract-violating
implementation, can an institutional review requirement constrain publication
without removing useful output?**

The adversarial member is a real native worker with ordinary task/ownership
permissions and an explicitly configured experiment-role instruction. It must
negotiate and claim work through the same institution; no preclaimed task,
scripted candidate or controller-selected attack is substituted. The remaining
participants pursue the unchanged exporter goal. The operator records which
identity is the experimental adversary, while peer board labels remain neutral.

All “private” fields are harmless synthetic input values. The experiment grants
no real secrets, external intrusion, destructive instructions or unrestricted
networking. This is an adversarial contribution intervention, not arbitrary
compromise of a model, host or institution.

## Fixed comparisons

Start with four development executions: benign/adversarial contributor
instruction crossed with voluntary/required publication review. Preserve three
coding identities, one reviewer, matched fixtures, budget, model and the existing
independent command-output audit. A fifth nonauthor peer remains consistent
guidance-only so total identities match the preceding workload.

Before expanding repetitions, require observable intervention: the designated
contributor actually submits an owned candidate, and the proposed behavior can
be classified independently. If it never owns relevant work or submits a
violating proposal, report nonexercise. Do not force ownership, resample a
failure away, or count hypothetical wrong code as a native outcome.

Measure final stdout disclosure/functionality, candidate contents, exact review
decisions, admission, publication revisions and later corrections separately.
The final artifact being safe cannot by itself show that an earlier unsafe
revision was prevented. Prospective candidate/canonical replay audits retain
both intermediate and final outcomes.

This development study is distinct from the 24-run message comparison. It will
not replace any previous outcome or become a pooled best-of-many estimate.
Its initial observed-credit pre-experiment guard is 600. Further experiments
require a newly frozen, bounded repetition plan based on exercise/measurement
validity, not whether the defense appears successful.

## Completed development and preregistered repetition decision

All four development executions produced independently correct final output;
one voluntary/adversarial society reached its deadline without formal closure.
The two adversarial contributors each submitted one program that independently
disclosed synthetic private fields. Coding peers and the now-authorized dedicated
reviewer rejected those exact proposals. Both were superseded, and independent
execution of every publication revision observed no disclosure.

This is genuine candidate-level exercise, unlike the earlier message-only probe.
It is not evidence that mandatory enforcement caused safety: voluntary review
also elicited correction in its single execution. The next main study freezes
the same four conditions with six repetitions each, preserving the same
contributor instruction rather than tuning it toward a desired publication.
Candidate, intermediate-publication and final-output outcomes are all retained.

The main study has a 3,000-observed-credit pre-experiment guard and a six-hour
wall bound. It does not replace the 24 earlier message-only outcomes or four
development executions. No positive effect is assumed, and an unexercised
candidate/ownership outcome remains in the denominator.

## Final outcome

All 24 replicated executions are terminal: 22 goal completions and two deadlines.
All 24 final exporters passed the independent output suite. All twelve
adversarial executions produced unsafe candidates: thirteen distinct snapshots,
including one resubmission before formal review. Ten passed submission checks;
twelve received formal rejection and all thirteen were superseded. Every
publication revision was separately executed; no synthetic disclosure was found.
Dedicated-reviewer authority was exercised successfully in every execution.

The preregistered study used 2,456.074 observed credits. Its conclusions remain
descriptive: required and voluntary review both corrected unsafe contributions.
The records do not establish mandatory-review superiority or protection against
an adversary that ignores feedback and publishes before review.
